# Contribution and attribution matrix

This matrix includes the constrained small-noise frontier. The checked source loci and applicability limits appear in `notes/novelty_review.md` and `notes/novelty_challenge.md`. `CONTRIBUTION_OVERLAP.md` retains an internal comparison with the supplied draft, which the author does not plan to submit. A changed premise does not by itself establish substantial originality.

| Manuscript result | Established antecedent or tool | Defensible claim and remaining risk |
|---|---|---|
| Scalar exactness to local moment factorization | [Wagstaff et al., Lemma 17 and its topological application](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume23/21-0730/21-0730.pdf); invariance of domain | Target-specific completion and local information reversal support full encoder classification. The reduction's significance and priority require specialist assessment. |
| Continuous affine-feature classification | [Charalambides, Theorem 1.6 and Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/1203.5370v2) | Self-contained proof from the stated one-way collisions. Affine identification and regularization are established principles. |
| Exact score and global peak synthesis | [ChannelComp, Section IV-A](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06532); dual norms and Schur complements | Classification makes the continuum pointwise-peak problem global over continuous shared planar encoders. The earlier finite modulation-vector constraint has a different feasible class. |
| Six-variable inverse-metric synthesis | Classical enclosing-ellipsoid and semidefinite modeling | A simpler equivalent representation, not a new generic optimization method. The seven-variable Gram form remains useful for certificates. |
| Five-variable constrained small-noise frontier | Weighted A-optimal criteria; [Sagnol, Section 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0912.5467); [Papp, Example 6](https://arxiv.org/html/1009.1444v2) | Computation-specific global synthesis, unique optimal Gram matrix, and a plateau followed by an active increasing branch. Inverse-trace optimization itself is established. |
| Explicit quadratic frontier | Symmetry, peak-boundary domination, scalar optimization | The proof covers the full continuous exact-encoder class. Its value extends beyond optimizing a stipulated one-parameter family. |
| Explicit quadratic and quartic separation optima | [Earlier analytical constellation design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06372); convex certificates | Different nonlinear targets and competitor classes. No first analytical-design claim; the quartic target is a Jensen gap. |
| Certified exponential example | Interpolation remainder, exact arithmetic, semidefinite weak duality | Reproducible continuum certificate for this formulation; no new interval-arithmetic principle. |
| Sharp explicit decoder | Classical scalar extension and nonexpansive projection | Metric strip projection realizes the computation-specific constant. |
| Qualitative approximate rigidity | Arzelà–Ascoli and exact rigidity | Supporting corollary under common encoder and decoder regularity; no explicit rate or implication from small average MSE. |
| Quantization and residual-gain bounds | Triangle inequality and the sharp decoder constant | Operational consequences with explicit assumptions; no exact unequal-channel classification. |
| Supplied bivariate companion | Shared peak-constrained MAC, parabolic maps, and computation-sensitive separation | Its K=2 uncentered-exponential theorems and fixed-family Bayesian ordering do not contain TIT's higher-arity classification or constrained small-noise frontier. The author confirmed that this is a draft not planned for submission; its citation has been removed from the current paper. |
| Numerical evidence and second implementation | Paired simulation and independently coded checks | Supports particular claims; proves neither unrestricted finite-noise Bayesian optimality nor priority. |

The strongest positioning is the full chain from scalar computation to necessary features, exact peak design, implemented protection, and a global constrained small-noise frontier. The current reviews document distinctions and attribution; they do not certify historical priority.
