\subsection{Robustness--MSE Tradeoff}
\label{sec:pareto-evidence}
With the encoder and decoder implementations checked, we quantify the average-error benefit of relaxing the uniform guarantee. We evaluate Corollary~\ref{cor:quadratic-pareto} for variance, $P=1$, and independent uniform or endpoint-Beta sources, where the latter means $s=2X-1$ with $X\sim\operatorname{Beta}(0.3,0.3)$. We use $K\in\{4,16\}$ and write $\eta=\gamma/\lambda_\star$ for the required fraction of optimal squared separation. Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} displays the exact small-noise frontier and its enclosing-ellipse geometry.



For uniform sources, the prior optimum retains $83.91\%$ of separation at $K=4$ and $60.91\%$ at $K=16$. A $90\%$ floor is active in both cases and selects the same encoder coefficients $a=0.9038538$, $b=1.4278414$, and $c=-1$. The source-dependent small-noise reductions are $17.68\%$ and $29.07\%$, respectively, while the sharp disturbance factor increases by $5.41\%$. For the endpoint-Beta prior at $K=4$, the unconstrained prior optimum already retains $91.67\%$, so a $90\%$ floor remains inactive.

We also evaluate eight prescribed retentions per scenario at $P/N_0=20$ dB, using $200{,}000$ independent source/noise draws per scenario and shared draws across encoders. Table~\ref{tab:pareto} compares the leading-coefficient prediction with measured gains at the $90\%$ floor. We report nominal pointwise $95\%$ confidence intervals for the relative MSE reduction using the paired delta method. Their finite-noise values assess these specified designs; the theorem concerns the limiting coefficient.

Twelve comparisons with a separately parameterized convex problem agree to $1.04\times10^{-9}$ relative objective error. Two runs report line-search termination despite residuals within $2.41\times10^{-10}$; the package records these consistency checks, not certificates. A second implementation reproduces the two uniform $90\%$ points, the exponential rational certificate, sixteen coefficient/moment cases, and a nondiagonal decoder comparison. These separate implementations use the same environment; external reproduction remains distinct.

The close agreement at $20$ dB supports the small-noise interpretation for these scenarios. A wider noise-power sweep is needed to examine the influence of clipping and nonlinear receiver postprocessing away from that regime.
