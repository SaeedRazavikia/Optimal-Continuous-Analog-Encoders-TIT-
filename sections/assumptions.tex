\subsection{Assumptions}\label{sec:assumptions}
We distinguish the assumptions for exact-encoder characterization from those for sharp separation and design:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{R}:\quad&\phi,\psi\in C(I)\cap C^1((-1,1)),\quad\phi\text{ nonaffine},\\
&\psi\text{ strictly convex or strictly concave};\\
\mathrm{S}:\quad&\mathrm{R},\quad\phi\text{ strictly convex or strictly concave},\quad\psi\in C^1(I).
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $C^1(I)$ requires finite continuous one-sided derivatives at both endpoints. Individual results state their additional assumptions; the two-statistic extension has its own identification condition.

\paragraph{Endpoint slopes}
Regime R alone need not give positive separation. For $\phi(s)=s^2$ and $\psi(s)=-\sqrt{1-s}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} forces every exact encoder to be an affine quadratic curve. The equal-input configurations $s_k=1$ and $s_k=1-h$ have received difference $O(h)$ but desired-function difference $\sqrt h-2h+h^2$, so their squared-separation quotient tends to zero. Nonexact encoders also have zero score by \eqref{eq:score}. Regime S excludes this endpoint singularity and permits the sharp sensitivity formula.
