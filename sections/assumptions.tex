\subsection{Assumptions}
Table~\ref{tab:assumptions} separates the classification assumptions from those needed for sharp optimization. In particular, differentiability in the interval interior does not provide a finite disturbance guarantee at the endpoints. Strict convexity or concavity of the feature is a sufficient condition for the endpoint rank argument; we do not claim that it is necessary.

\paragraph{Endpoint slopes}
Consider $\phi(s)=s^2$ and $\psi(s)=-\sqrt{1-s}$. Both satisfy the rigidity hypotheses, but $\psi'$ diverges at $1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} makes every exact continuous encoder an affine quadratic curve. Compare the two equal-input configurations $s_k=1$ and $s_k=1-h$, where $h\downarrow0$. Their received difference is
$$
\Delta r=K[-hu+(-2h+h^2)v]=O(h),
$$
whereas their target difference is $\sqrt h-2h+h^2$. Their squared-separation quotient therefore tends to zero. Every exact encoder has zero score, and nonexact encoders have zero score by definition. Thus the rigidity assumptions alone permit a zero global optimum and no decoder with finite uniform disturbance amplification.
