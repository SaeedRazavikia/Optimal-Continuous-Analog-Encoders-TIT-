\section{Optimal Curves}\label{sec:examples}

We now turn the convex characterization into explicit encoder curves. Figure~\ref{fig:curves} shows how the prescribed local feature and target function determine the optimal scale, translation, and peak-contact pattern.

\subsection{Quadratic Features}
Set $\phi(s)=s^2$ and let $\psi\in C^1(I)$ be even and strictly convex or strictly concave.
We set $M=|\psi'(1)|>0$ and $t=M/2$, and define
\begin{align*}
 b&=\frac{2}{1-t^2+t\sqrt{t^2+2}},\\
 a&=\sqrt{b(2-b)}.
\end{align*}
For numerical evaluation, the equivalent denominator $1+2t/(\sqrt{t^2+2}+t)$ avoids cancellation at large $t$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:parabola}
For every $K\ge4$, an optimal encoder for $f=Q/K-\psi(S/K)$ over all continuous peak-admissible maps is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \enc^\star(s)&=\sqrt P\,[a s+i(bs^2-1)],\\
 \Lambda_{\sq}^\star(f)&=K^2P\,b^2(b-1).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:parabola}
\end{equation}
Thus an infinite-dimensional class of nonlinear mean-dependent terms has an explicit optimal encoder determined by $M$ alone.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Nonexact encoders have zero score. For every exact encoder, rigidity gives a nondegenerate quadratic curve. Since $\psi$ is even, Theorem~\ref{thm:sdp} gives
$$
 \frac{\Lambda_{\sq}}{K^2}
 =\frac{AB-C^2}{A+M^2B+2M|C|}.
$$
Normalize $P=1$. The stated parameter satisfies $1<b<2$ and
$$M^2b(b-1)=(2-b)^2.$$
We prove the converse directly from the peak constraints. Averaging those at $\pm1$ gives $A+|c+v|^2\le1$, while the constraint at zero gives $|c|^2\le1$. Set $L=bA+(b-1)B$ and $D=A+M^2B$. The identity
\begin{align*}
 L+|bc+v|^2
 =b\bigl(A+|c+v|^2\bigr)+b(b-1)|c|^2\le b^2
\end{align*}
implies $L\le b^2$. The parameter equation then yields the certificate
\begin{align*}
 b(b-1)DL-bAB
 &=(b-1)\bigl[bA-(2-b)B\bigr]^2\ge0.
\end{align*}
Hence $AB\le b^2(b-1)D$. Subtracting $C^2$ from the numerator and adding $2M|C|$ to the denominator preserves the bound, proving $\Lambda_{\sq}/K^2\le b^2(b-1)$.

The proposed curve has $A=b(2-b)$, $B=b^2$, and $C=0$. It attains equality and satisfies the full peak identity
$$
 |\enc_b(s)|^2=1-b^2s^2(1-s^2)\le1\qquad(s\in[-1,1]).
$$
Scaling by $\sqrt P$ restores the stated power and score. The parameter formula follows by solving its quadratic equation; its unique solution in $(1,2)$ also has the stable form
$$b=\frac{2\bigl(\sqrt{M^2+8}+M\bigr)}{\sqrt{M^2+8}+3M}.$$
\end{proof}

Examples include $\psi(m)=m^{2r}$ for every integer $r\ge1$, $\psi(m)=-\eta m^{2r}$ for $\eta>0$, and $\psi(m)=m^2+\eta m^4$ for $\eta\ge0$. In the last case $M=2+4\eta$. Strict convexity permits a vanishing second derivative at isolated points, so $m^4$ satisfies the theorem. More generally, different even strictly convex mean functions with the same endpoint slope share the same optimal geometry.

For variance, $M=2$ and $b=2/\sqrt3$, so
$$
 \Lambda_{\sq}^\star(V)=\frac{4K^2P}{9+6\sqrt3}.
$$
The baseline $\sqrt P[s+i(s^2-1)]$ has score $K^2P/5$, so the improvement is $3.1337\%$ in squared separation. This is not an MSE improvement. We formalize its operational score and optimum over all quadratic coefficient curves for every $P>0$ and $K\ge2$ in Lean~4; Appendix~\ref{app:lean} states the scope and the remaining continuous-encoder reduction.

\subsection{Quartic Features}
We next set $\phi(s)=s^4$ and again assume $\psi$ is even and strictly convex or strictly concave. Put $M=|\psi'(1)|$. Let $\rho$ be the unique solution in $(0,1/3)$ of
\begin{equation}
 8\rho^2(1+\rho)(1+3\rho)=M^2(1-3\rho).
 \label{eq:quarticroot}
\end{equation}
The left side increases strictly from zero, and the right side decreases strictly to zero on this interval. Define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 d_\rho&=1+2\rho+9\rho^2,\qquad
 a_\rho=\sqrt{\frac{8\rho}{d_\rho}},\\
 b_\rho&=\frac{2}{(1+\rho)\sqrt{d_\rho}},\qquad
 c_\rho=-\frac{1+2\rho+3\rho^2}{(1+\rho)\sqrt{d_\rho}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:quarticcoefficients}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:quartic}
For every $K\ge4$, an optimal continuous encoder for $K^{-1}\sum_k s_k^4-\psi(S/K)$ is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\enc^\star(s)=\sqrt P\,[a_\rho s+i(b_\rho s^4+c_\rho)]}.
 \label{eq:quarticmap}
\end{equation}
Its optimal squared separation is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Lambda_{\sq}^\star
 =\frac{K^2P}{M^2/a_\rho^2+1/b_\rho^2}
 =\frac{8K^2P\rho}
 {d_\rho\,[M^2+2\rho(1+\rho)^2]}}.
 \label{eq:quarticscore}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
Reflection reduces the design to orthogonal coefficients, and two active peak constraints certify global optimality. Appendix~\ref{app:quartic-proof} gives the proof.

For the fourth-order Jensen gap, $\psi(m)=m^4$ and $M=4$. Equation~\eqref{eq:quarticroot} becomes
$$
 3\rho^4+4\rho^3+\rho^2+6\rho-2=0,
 \qquad\rho\simeq0.297207855283.
$$
Hence an optimal map and value are
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Re}\enc^\star(s)/\sqrt P&\simeq0.997539156206\,s,\\
 \operatorname{Im}\enc^\star(s)/\sqrt P&\simeq0.997413500602\,s^4-0.927301933535,
\end{align*}
$$
 \Lambda_{\sq}^\star\simeq0.058533506998\,K^2P.
$$
The formulas defining the root and coefficients are exact; these decimals are for interpretation. Four source values use full peak energy: $s=\pm1$ and $s=\pm\sqrt\rho$. The center has strictly smaller energy. This differs from the optimal quadratic map, whose contacts occur at $-1,0,1$.


\subsection{Moment Interaction}
The interaction $f=mq$ illustrates a change of readout within the same quadratic feature family. Under the same continuous shared-encoder model and $K\ge4$, we obtain
$$
 \enc^\star(s)=\sqrt P\left[\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}s
 +i\left(\frac43s^2-1\right)\right],\qquad
 \Lambda_{\sq}^\star=\frac{16}{27}K^2P.
$$
Theorem~\ref{thm:polynomial} in Appendix~\ref{app:polynomial} proves this result for a larger, explicitly restricted polynomial readout class. The extension changes the optimal scales through the endpoint derivatives of the readout; it introduces no additional additive statistic. We use the quartic and nonpolynomial examples to demonstrate changes in the feature curve itself.
