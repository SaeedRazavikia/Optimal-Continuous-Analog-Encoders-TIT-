\subsection{Finite-Noise Performance}\label{sec:finite-noise}
We vary the noise power for variance and the fourth-order Jensen gap, using the projection decoder of Section~\ref{sec:decoding} followed by clipping to $[0,1]$. For $p\in\{2,4\}$, write
$$
 f_p=t_p-m^p,\qquad
 \enc(s)=\sqrt P\,[as+i(bs^p+c)],\quad a,b>0.
$$
Let $N=\sqrt{N_0}Z$, where $Z$ is circular Gaussian, independent of the sources, with $\mathbb E|Z|^2=1$; each real component of $N$ has variance $N_0/2$.

For independent sources with a common continuous density on $(-1,1)$, the desired function $f_p:I^K\to\R$ takes values strictly inside $(0,1)$ almost surely. Proposition~\ref{prop:general-small-noise} therefore specializes to
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{N_0\downarrow0}\frac{\operatorname{MSE}(\enc,N_0)}{N_0}
 =\frac{1}{2K^2P}\left(\frac{1}{b^2}
 +\frac{p^2\mathbb E[m^{2p-2}]}{a^2}\right).
 \label{eq:small-noise-mse}
\end{equation}


The terms in \eqref{eq:small-noise-mse} correspond to errors in the empirical moment $t_p$ and sample mean $m$. The worst-case criterion uses $M=p$, whereas the small-noise coefficient uses $M_{\rm eff}=p\sqrt{\mathbb E[m^{2p-2}]}$. Substituting $M_{\rm eff}$ into the quadratic or quartic formulas minimizes this coefficient within the stated orthogonal family. We compute the required moments analytically and use the same decoder for all designs. This prior-aware comparison does not optimize finite-noise Bayesian risk; Section~\ref{sec:pareto} constrains the tradeoff with uniform robustness.




We set $P=1$ and $K\in\{4,16\}$. For each $K$ and uniform or endpoint-Beta prior, we generate $200{,}000$ independent input vectors and independent standardized Gaussian disturbances. We share these draws across encoders, desired functions, and nine SNR values from $-10$ to $30$ dB in $5$-dB steps. SNR denotes the per-transmitter peak ratio $P/N_0$. Paired standard errors account for shared draws; confidence intervals are pointwise, without multiple-comparison adjustment.

Across the 72 combinations of desired function, prior, $K$, and SNR, separation-optimal curves reduce MSE by $6.12\%$--$22.92\%$ from the baselines; prior-aware curves give a further $5.69\%$--$72.01\%$ reduction (Fig.~\ref{fig:finite-noise} and Table~\ref{tab:finite-noise}). These gains sacrifice robustness: for the quartic gap with uniform sources and $K=16$, prior-aware tuning reduces $\Lambda_\sq/(K^2P)$ from $0.05853$ to $0.007273$.

At $-10$ dB, for uniform sources, $K=4$, and variance, the prior-mean estimator has MSE $0.02294$, versus $0.1032$ for the separation-optimal curve and implemented decoder. Thus these comparisons establish neither universal MSE dominance nor Bayesian optimality.

At $30$ dB, endpoint-Beta sources with $K=4$ give normalized errors $3.49\%$--$7.19\%$ below their small-noise limits, beyond Monte Carlo uncertainty; boundary clipping remains relevant. The code records all conditions, paired uncertainties, analytic prior moments, seeds, and decoder cross-checks.
