\section{Decoding}\label{sec:decoding}
Identifiable computation requires a decoder that recovers the desired function on the noiseless received set. Noise can move the observation outside that set, so a receiver also needs a rule there. Extending the noiseless inverse naively may amplify disturbances without bound; the variance inverse, for example, contains a squared coordinate. We first relate squared separation to the best uniform noise bound, then construct a projection decoder that attains it.

% \subsection{Uniform Error Bound}
For a fixed encoder, define $\mathcal A(\enc;f)$ as the infimum of constants $L\ge0$ for which one decoder $\widehat f:\R^2\to\R$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 |\widehat f(r_\enc(\mathbf s)+n)-f(\mathbf s)|\le L|n|
 \quad\forall\mathbf s\in I^K,\ \forall n\in\R^2.
 \label{eq:uniform}
\end{equation}
Set $\mathcal A=+\infty$ if no finite constant exists; one fixed decoder must handle every input and disturbance.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:amplification}
Let $I=[-1,1]$, $K\in\mathbb N$ with $K\ge1$, $\enc:I\to\R^2$ be continuous, and $f:I^K\to\R$ be continuous and nonconstant. Then
$$\mathcal A(\enc;f)=\Lambda_\sq(\enc;f)^{-1/2},$$
with $0^{-1/2}=+\infty$. A continuous decoder attains the infimum whenever it is finite.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The choice $n=0$ requires noiseless recovery and hence the identifiability condition. Using one noiseless received point as the observation under another input gives $|\Delta f|\le L|\Delta r|$, which proves the lower bound. Conversely, a finite supremum of $|\Delta f|/|\Delta r|$ defines a Lipschitz receiver function $d_0:\mathcal R_\enc\to\R$ on the compact noiseless received set $\mathcal R_\enc$. McShane's construction~\cite{mcshane1934},
$$\widehat f(y)=\inf_{z\in\mathcal R_\enc}\{d_0(z)+L|y-z|\},$$
extends $d_0$ with the same constant. The triangle inequality proves global Lipschitz continuity; agreement with $d_0$ on $\mathcal R_\enc$ then gives \eqref{eq:uniform}. Clipping the scalar output to any interval containing $f(I^K)$ preserves the guarantee.
\end{proof}
Our encoder optimum determines the smallest extension constant. For an explicit decoder, we enforce $m\in[-1,1]$ on the recovered statistics. Write $\enc(s)=c+us+v\phi(s)$, let $\mathsf U=[u\ v]\in\R^{2\times2}$ be invertible with $G=\mathsf U^\T\mathsf U$, and define
$$
 (\widetilde m,\widetilde t)^\T=\frac1K \mathsf U^{-1}(y-Kc).
$$
We project in metric $G$ onto the strip $\mathcal S=[-1,1]\times\R$. Its closed-form solution gives the decoder
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}
 \widehat m&=\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}(\widetilde m),\\
 \widehat t&=\widetilde t+\frac{G_{12}}{G_{22}}(\widetilde m-\widehat m),\\
 \widehat f(y)&=\widehat t-\psi(\widehat m).
\end{aligned}\label{eq:stripdecoder}\end{equation}
Decoding uses a fixed linear transformation, scalar clipping, and function evaluation; nonorthogonal $u,v$ require the correction in $\widehat t$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:decoder}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:sdp}, let $\enc(s)=c+us+v\phi(s)$ with $c,u,v\in\R^2$ and linearly independent $u,v$. The receiver function $\widehat f:\R^2\to\R$ in \eqref{eq:stripdecoder} recovers $f_{\phi,\psi}:I^K\to\R$ without noise and attains the smallest possible uniform constant for this encoder
\begin{equation}
 L_\enc=\frac1K\sqrt{\max_{\sigma\in\{-,+\}}w_\sigma^\T G^{-1}w_\sigma}
 =\Lambda_\sq(\enc;f)^{-1/2}.
 \label{eq:decoderconstant}
\end{equation}
An additional scalar projection onto an interval containing $f(I^K)$ preserves this conclusion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $x=(m,t)^\T$ and $\widetilde x=(\widetilde m,\widetilde t)^\T$, Euclidean projection onto the received strip $Kc+K\mathsf U\mathcal S$ minimizes $K^2(x-\widetilde x)^\T G(x-\widetilde x)$. For fixed $m$, differentiation in $t$ gives
$$t=\widetilde t-G_{12}(m-\widetilde m)/G_{22}.$$
Substitution leaves the positive Schur-complement coefficient $G_{11}-G_{12}^2/G_{22}$ multiplying $(m-\widetilde m)^2$, so clipping $m$ proves \eqref{eq:stripdecoder}. Euclidean projection onto this closed convex received set is nonexpansive.

The receiver postprocessing function $F:\mathcal S\to\R$, $F(m,t)=t-\psi(m)$, has gradient $(-\psi'(m),1)^\T$. The endpoint-slope argument for \eqref{eq:exactscore} bounds its squared dual norm in metric $G$ by $K^2L_\enc^2$. Integration along segments proves that the corresponding receiver function on $Kc+K\mathsf U\mathcal S\subset\R^2$ is $L_\enc$-Lipschitz. Its composition with nonexpansive projection preserves this constant and fixes all noiseless points. The exact score and Proposition~\ref{prop:amplification} prove optimality.
\end{proof}

For variance at $K=4$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:decoder-geometry}), the attainable moment region lies between $q=m^2$ and $q=[3+(4m-j)^2]/4$, where $j\in\{-3,-1,1,3\}$ and $|4m-j|\le1$. Maximizing the convex second moment on a fixed-mean cube slice gives the upper boundary: vertices have at most one nonendpoint coordinate. Connectedness gives every intermediate moment. This generally nonconvex region lies inside the strip, which admits explicit nonexpansive projection.

For $\phi(s)=s^p$, $p\in\{2,4\}$, a tighter containing set is
$$\mathcal C_p=\{(m,t):-1\le m\le1,\ m^p\le t\le1\}.$$
Jensen's inequality gives containment. Projection onto its convex received image preserves the guarantee but requires convex optimization. This convex hull need not equal the finite-$K$ moment set.

For the optimal orthogonal map $\sqrt P[a s+i(bs^p+c_0)]$, compute
\begin{align*}
 \widehat m&=\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}\frac{\operatorname{Re}y}{Ka\sqrt P},\\
 \widehat t&=\frac{\operatorname{Im}y/(K\sqrt P)-c_0}{b}.
\end{align*}
The variance and fourth-order Jensen-gap decoders are $\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}(\widehat t-\widehat m^p)$, with the appropriate $p$ and coefficients. Both desired functions take values in $[0,1]$.

\subsection{Moment Interactions and Operational Limits}
For the moment interaction $f(\mathbf s)=m q$, projection onto the containing rectangle $[-1,1]\times[0,1]$ under the optimal orthogonal curve gives
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}
 \widehat m&=\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}\frac{3\operatorname{Re}y}{2\sqrt2K\sqrt P},\\
 \widehat q&=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\frac34\left(\frac{\operatorname{Im}y}{K\sqrt P}+1\right),\\
 \widehat f_{mq}(y)&=\widehat m\widehat q.
\end{aligned}\label{eq:mqdecoder}\end{equation}
Orthogonality makes the received projection coordinatewise clipping. For $a=2\sqrt2/3$, $b=4/3$, the dual gradient norm of $mq$ is at most $\sqrt{1/a^2+1/b^2}=3\sqrt3/4$, yielding the sharp constant $L_{mq}=3\sqrt3/(4K\sqrt P)$. The restricted polynomial class satisfies its endpoint partial-derivative bounds on the same rectangle, so projection followed by polynomial postprocessing also attains its sharp constant.


Projection controls the raw inverse outside the noiseless received set: the raw variance and fourth-order inverses contain $(\operatorname{Re}y)^2$ and $(\operatorname{Re}y)^4$, respectively, and are not globally Lipschitz. For a fixed encoder and known disturbance radius $\tau\ge0$, define
$$\omega_\enc(d)=\max_{\substack{\mathbf s,\mathbf t\in I^K\\|r_\enc(\mathbf s)-r_\enc(\mathbf t)|\le d}}|f(\mathbf s)-f(\mathbf t)|,\qquad d\ge0.$$
The minimum worst-case absolute error at radius $\tau$ equals $\omega_\enc(2\tau)/2$. Two noiseless received sums at distance at most $2\tau$ can give the same midpoint observation under admissible disturbances, proving the lower bound. A matching decoder outputs the midpoint of the smallest and largest values of $f(\mathbf s)$ consistent with the observation and radius. Compactness gives these extrema. Their maximum is upper semicontinuous and minimum lower semicontinuous on the compact possible-observation set, so the midpoint rule has a Borel measurable extension. This criterion depends on the full ambiguity profile, rather than one constant for every disturbance size.

For any finite-second-moment noise, \eqref{eq:uniform} implies
$$\mathbb E|\widehat f(Y)-f(\mathbf s)|^2\le L_\enc^2\mathbb E|N|^2,$$
also after averaging over an input prior. Under $N\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,N_0)$, the bound is $L_\enc^2N_0$. This is a bound for a specified decoder, not unrestricted Gaussian-MSE optimality.
