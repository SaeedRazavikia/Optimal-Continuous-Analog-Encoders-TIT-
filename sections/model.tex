\section{System Model}\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Multiple-Access Channel}
In Fig.~\ref{fig:system}, each transmitter $k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$ holds a calibrated real observation $s_k\in I=[-1,1]\subset\R$, where $K\in\mathbb N$ and $K\ge1$. We collect these observations in the input vector $\mathbf s=(s_1,\ldots,s_K)^\T\in I^K\subset\R^K$. The transmitters apply a shared continuous encoder $\enc:I\to\C$ over one simultaneous complex channel use. We identify $\C$ with $\R^2$, whose components are the in-phase and quadrature coordinates. The inputs and desired function are real valued. Unless stated otherwise, converse and optimal-design results assume $K\ge4$.

For a given $P>0$, we impose the per-transmitter pointwise power constraint
\begin{equation}
 x_k=\enc(s_k),\qquad \sup_{s\in I}|\enc(s)|^2\le P.
 \label{eq:peak-model}
\end{equation}
With synchronized transmissions and unit effective channel gains after compensation, the receiver observes
\begin{equation}
 Y=\sum_{k=1}^{K}x_k+N=r_\enc(\mathbf s)+N,
 \qquad \widehat f(Y)=D(Y),
 \label{eq:mac-model}
\end{equation}
where $r_\enc:I^K\to\C$, $r_\enc(\mathbf s)=\sum_{k=1}^K\enc(s_k)$, is the noiseless superposition and $D:\C\to\R$ is the receiver. For uniform error bounds, $N\in\C$ is arbitrary. For MSE analysis, we assume $N\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,N_0)$ independent of $\mathbf s$, with complex variance $N_0=\mathbb E|N|^2$, the noise power per channel use; each real component has variance $N_0/2$. We define the per-transmitter peak SNR as $P/N_0$. Our high-SNR limit takes $N_0\downarrow0$ at fixed $P$, $K$, encoder, and input distribution. The one-use payload count excludes calibration and synchronization overhead. Section~\ref{sec:implementation} bounds residual implementation and channel errors.

\subsection{Desired Functions and Examples}
We fix the input calibration and desired function before designing the encoder. For continuous real-valued functions $\phi,\psi:I\to\R$, we consider $f_{\phi,\psi}:I^K\to\R$ defined by
\begin{equation}
 f_{\phi,\psi}(\mathbf s)
 =\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K\phi(s_k)
   -\psi\!\left(\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^Ks_k\right),
 \qquad \mathbf s\in I^K.
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
Here $\phi$ preprocesses local observations and $\psi$ acts on the empirical mean. Define $S=\sum_k s_k$, $T=\sum_k\phi(s_k)$, $m=S/K\in I$, and $t=T/K\in\R$, so $f_{\phi,\psi}(\mathbf s)=t-\psi(m)$. For integer $p\ge1$, write $t_p=K^{-1}\sum_k s_k^p$ for the empirical $p$th raw moment; in particular, $Q=\sum_k s_k^2$ and $q=Q/K=t_2$.

\emph{Mean.} With $\phi(s)=s$ and $\psi\equiv0$, the encoder $\enc(s)=\sqrt P\,s$ and noiseless receiver $D(y)=\operatorname{Re}(y)/(K\sqrt P)$ compute $f(\mathbf s)=m$ from one real statistic. This case of \eqref{eq:target} does not satisfy our converse's nonaffineness and strict-convexity assumptions.

\emph{Variance.} Choosing $\phi(s)=\psi(s)=s^2$ gives the empirical variance
$$
 V(\mathbf s)=q-m^2=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K(s_k-m)^2.
$$
For example, $\enc(s)=\sqrt{P/2}\,(s+i s^2)$ obeys \eqref{eq:peak-model}. In the absence of noise, $Y_0=r_\enc(\mathbf s)$ yields
$$
 m=\frac{\operatorname{Re}Y_0}{K\sqrt{P/2}},\qquad
 q=\frac{\operatorname{Im}Y_0}{K\sqrt{P/2}},\qquad V=q-m^2.
$$
Section~\ref{sec:examples} optimizes the scaling and translation of this two-moment curve.

\emph{Jensen gaps.} Convex $\psi=\phi$ gives the empirical Jensen gap. For $\phi(s)=s^4$ and $e^s$, this is $t_4-m^4$ and $K^{-1}\sum_k e^{s_k}-e^m$, respectively. The fourth central moment also involves the second and third raw moments and differs from $t_4-m^4$. More generally, bounded $\phi$ gives the feasible encoder $\enc(s)=\alpha[s+i\phi(s)]$, $\alpha=\sqrt{P/(1+\|\phi\|_\infty^2)}>0$, showing that $S,T$ determine the desired function. Our converse identifies when every shared continuous encoder that permits noiseless recovery must preserve both sums.

\emph{Relation to nomographic functions.} A scalar nomographic representation has the form
$$
 f(\mathbf s)=\Phi\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^K h_k(s_k)\right),
$$
where $h_k:I\to\R$ are local preprocessing functions and $\Phi$ postprocesses attainable sums~\cite{goldenbaum2015}. We use two real statistics, $(S,T)\in\R^2$, followed by $F(m,t)=t-\psi(m)$, with $(m,t)=(S/K,T/K)$: a two-coordinate nomographic construction within one complex symbol. The classes overlap, as the mean illustrates. Under Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}'s assumptions, no shared continuous scalar encoder $h:I\to\R$ permits identifiable computation of \eqref{eq:target}, because two linearly independent real coefficient vectors are necessary. This restriction does not exclude transmitter-dependent or discontinuous preprocessing.

\subsection{Identifiable Computation and Separation}
For a specified continuous desired function $f:I^K\to\R$, an encoder $\enc$ permits \emph{identifiable computation} if its noiseless received sum uniquely determines $f(\mathbf s)$. Formally, we require
\begin{equation}
 \forall\,\mathbf s,\mathbf t\in I^K,\qquad
 r_\enc(\mathbf s)=r_\enc(\mathbf t)
 \ \Longrightarrow\ f(\mathbf s)=f(\mathbf t).
 \label{eq:exactness}
\end{equation}
Condition~\eqref{eq:exactness} is the \emph{identifiability condition for $f$}. It requires unequal function values to have different received sums, while allowing input vectors with the same function value to produce the same sum. Equivalently, there exists a noiseless receiver $D_0:\mathcal R_\enc\to\R$, $\mathcal R_\enc=r_\enc(I^K)\subset\C$, such that $D_0(r_\enc(\mathbf s))=f(\mathbf s)$ for every $\mathbf s\in I^K$. It is continuous because the continuous surjection from compact $I^K$ onto Hausdorff $\mathcal R_\enc$ is a quotient map. Identifiability alone imposes no quantitative bound on sensitivity to noise. To control that sensitivity, we compare distances between superpositions representing unequal function values.

For a nonconstant $f:I^K\to\R$, we define the squared-separation score
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_{\sq}(\enc;f)
 =\inf_{\substack{\mathbf s,\mathbf t\in I^K\\f(\mathbf s)\ne f(\mathbf t)}}
 \frac{|r_\enc(\mathbf s)-r_\enc(\mathbf t)|^2}
 {|f(\mathbf s)-f(\mathbf t)|^2}\in[0,\infty).
 \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
A positive score guarantees identifiability of $f$ and $|f(\mathbf s)-f(\mathbf t)|\le |r_\enc(\mathbf s)-r_\enc(\mathbf t)|/\sqrt{\Lambda_{\sq}}$ for every $\mathbf s,\mathbf t\in I^K$. Proposition~\ref{prop:amplification} identifies $1/\sqrt{\Lambda_{\sq}}$ as the smallest achievable uniform noise-amplification constant.

We maximize \eqref{eq:score} over the class
$$
 \mathcal E_P=\{\enc\in C(I;\C):\sup_{s\in I}|\enc(s)|^2\le P\},\qquad
 \Lambda_{\sq}^\star(f)=\sup_{\enc\in\mathcal E_P}\Lambda_{\sq}(\enc;f).
$$
Under the assumptions below, Section~\ref{sec:design} proves $\enc(s)=c+us+v\phi(s)$ for every encoder satisfying \eqref{eq:exactness}, giving $r_\enc(\mathbf s)=Kc+uS+vT$. The offset $c$ positions the curve in the peak-power disk; the lengths and angle of $u,v$ control moment-recovery sensitivity. This reduces continuous-function optimization to finitely many coefficients.
