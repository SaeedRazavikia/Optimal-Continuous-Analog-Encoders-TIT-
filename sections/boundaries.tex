\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:boundaries}
For the moment-based functions considered here, requiring the received sum to identify the desired function fixes the continuous encoder's form. Its remaining geometric freedom can then be optimized under pointwise peak power. We establish this characterization for $f_{\phi,\psi}(\mathbf s)=t-\psi(m)$ and extend it to functions of two prescribed additive statistics under a common-completion condition. For the original function class, the resulting convex design and projection decoder attain a sharp uniform disturbance guarantee. Inverse-Gram coordinates also give the robustness-constrained small-noise frontier for the specified decoder, with an explicit quadratic specialization and certified numerical design for smooth preprocessing functions.

The classification assumes one synchronized complex channel use, unit effective gains, a shared encoder, and $K\ge4$. User-specific encoders, unequal uncompensated gains, average-power constraints, and unrestricted $K=3$ classification require further analysis. The two-statistic extension requires common-completion identification for classification, Lipschitz postprocessing for positive separation, and additional endpoint conditions for the explicit quadratic formula. It does not provide a universal representation of symmetric functions~\cite{wagstaff19,wagstaff22}.

For each encoder, the score gives the smallest uniform amplification over decoders that recover the desired function without noise. The statistical frontier concerns the specified strip decoder; finite-noise Bayesian optimization over arbitrary estimators remains open. Section~\ref{sec:implementation} bounds residual mismatch, while optimal design for unequal channels remains a separate problem.
