\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:boundaries}
We classify exact shared continuous planar encoders for the stated moment-and-mean targets and optimize their worst-case conditioning under pointwise peak power. The exact score, convex encoder design, and projection decoder turn that structural result into a sharp uniform disturbance guarantee. The inverse-metric description also yields the global robustness-constrained small-noise frontier for the specified decoder. Its quadratic specialization quantifies the cost of adapting to an input distribution, while certified discretization supplies a stopping rule for smooth features beyond the explicit cases.

The assumptions delimit the contribution. We use one synchronized complex channel use, unit effective channel gains, the same encoder at every transmitter, and at least four transmitters. Unequal uncompensated gains, user-specific encoders, average-power constraints, and unrestricted $K=3$ classification require different arguments. The target is fixed before design; the result does not cover arbitrary symmetric functions. The polynomial extension in Appendix~\ref{app:polynomial} retains substantive sign and parity restrictions. Universal sum representations have established dimension limitations~\cite{wagstaff19,wagstaff22}.

The score optimizes one decoder's uniform amplification over every input and disturbance. The constrained frontier minimizes its fixed-encoder leading MSE coefficient; finite-noise Bayesian optimization over arbitrary estimators remains a separate question. Section~\ref{sec:implementation} bounds residual mismatch without solving optimal design for unequal channels. Appendix~\ref{app:boundaries} gives precise boundaries for criterion transfer, poor conditioning, and finite alphabets. Appendix~\ref{app:approx-rigidity} proves qualitative stability under common regularity bounds, while an explicit error-to-geometry rate remains open. These restrictions identify concrete directions for extending the present classification and design theorem.
