\section{Lean Verification}\label{app:lean}

We verify selected results in Lean~4~\cite{lean4}, using mathlib~\cite{mathlib}. The artifact includes the proof sources, a theorem map, a complete manuscript coverage inventory, and reproducible compilation and axiom audits. It distinguishes complete results from specializations and conditional steps.

For the original family $f_{\phi,\psi}$, the collision lemma is verified for arbitrary local preprocessing functions $\phi$ and encoders and every $K\ge4$, without differentiability of $\psi$. The operational score formula \eqref{eq:exactscore} and its shrinking-pair clause are also verified for every encoder $\enc(s)=c+us+v\phi(s)$ with linearly independent $u,v$ and $K\ge2$, under the stated curvature assumptions on $\phi$ and endpoint regularity assumptions on $\psi$. The formal score is the infimum over actual admissible input pairs. The proof uses explicit two-input perturbations and one-sided endpoint derivatives.

For variance, the score identity additionally covers degenerate quadratic curves. Lean verifies the operational optimum over every quadratic coefficient curve at all positive powers:
$$
 \max_{\substack{\enc(s)=c+us+vs^2\\|\enc(s)|^2\le P\;\forall s\in I}}
 \Lambda_{\sq}(\enc;V)
 =\frac{4K^2P}{9+6\sqrt3},\qquad K\ge2.
$$
The complete quadratic and quartic Gram-objective optimizations, including continuum peak certificates and arbitrary positive slope parameter and power, are also checked. These coefficient-class theorems do not yet extend their competitors to every continuous encoder.

For encoder design in the original family, the artifact verifies the Schur-complement reduction, rank-one score constraints, and explicit planar reconstruction. The reconstruction is connected to the general operational score: a positive feasible SDP point yields a continuous encoder that identifies $f_{\phi,\psi}$ and satisfies the full peak constraint and the promised separation bound. The proof derives spanning anchors and a positive feasible point from the assumptions on $\phi$, establishes compactness, and proves maximum-separation attainment and its operational realization within the coefficient class $\enc(s)=c+us+v\phi(s)$.

For the extension in Theorem~\ref{thm:extension}, Lean verifies identifiability through invertible affine transformations of $\gamma$, the attained-pair score identity \eqref{eq:general-score-appendix}, and the equivalent constraints on the actual set $\mathcal M$. Under common-completion identification, it proves the triple-collision implication and derives $G\succ0$ from positive feasible separation. Functional independence supplies spanning anchors, and continuity and Lipschitz postprocessing supply a positive feasible design. Lean then proves compactness, positive maximum attainment, and planar realization with operational score $K^2\lambda_\gamma^\star$. These results optimize over all coefficient curves, including singular competitors; extending their converse to every continuous encoder still requires the unfinished rigidity reduction. The analytic identification criterion and general endpoint-gradient specializations also remain unverified.

For rigidity, Lean constructs local moment inverses and neighborhoods, derives kernel relations for derivatives from local fiber constancy, and integrates through both source endpoints. It now verifies automatic differentiability from a local continuous factorization using a compactly supported convolution and a local continuous extension. It also verifies continuous recovery on the actual compact received set. The proof propagates derivative relations from the dense identifying set and obtains an affine representation on the closed interval. The remaining converse must derive the required local continuous factorization from identifiability of $f$ and common-completion identification. Planar invariance of domain is not formalized here.

The decoder development verifies the sharp scalar amplification principle, including the zero-score impossibility result, and the actual general strip decoder with its correction for nonorthogonal coefficient vectors. It proves noiseless recovery, uniform error bounds, and optimality against arbitrary alternative decoders. The small-noise development derives the general decoder's derivative, proves dominated convergence, and evaluates the inverse-Gram trace coefficient for actual admissible inputs. It constructs the circular Gaussian law from two independent real Gaussian coordinates and proves the required moments. Locally inactive scalar output clipping preserves the limit. These results establish Proposition~\ref{prop:general-small-noise} within the coefficient class; the unrestricted continuous-encoder interpretation retains the rigidity dependency. Coherent implementation-error bounds and peak-safe contraction before quantization are also checked.

For probabilistic design, Lean verifies the attained Gram frontier, its uniqueness, convexity, monotonicity, plateau, activity, and strict-increase properties. The continuity proof includes both endpoints. The explicit quadratic frontier is checked at every positive power, including existence and uniqueness of the upper root, the clipped optimum, and both endpoint floors. The enclosing-ellipse development derives positive definiteness from the block constraints and proves the constructive correspondence with Gram designs, preserving the endpoint costs and attained separation optimum. These finite-dimensional results retain the separate continuous-encoder reduction.

The certification development verifies the sharp interpolation bound, finite-grid bracketing, sampled-SDP compactness and attainment, peak-restoring Gram scaling, the operator-norm derivative bound, and the resulting $O(h^2)$ scaling loss. It also proves dissimilarity transfer from actual shrinking input pairs. For approximate rigidity, Lean proves Arzel\`a--Ascoli compactness of the actual peak-constrained encoder class with a common modulus of continuity and constructs a nondecreasing error modulus tending to zero; its affine-family interpretation retains the dependency on the encoder characterization.
