\section{Scope of the Criterion}\label{app:boundaries}
The preceding optima concern squared separation on a continuous input interval. We now explain when changing the dissimilarity preserves those optima, why identifiable computation alone does not ensure good conditioning, and how restricting the input alphabet changes the problem.

\subsection{Identifiable Computation and Conditioning}
The family
$$\enc_\beta(s)=\sqrt P[s+i\beta(1-s^2)],\qquad0<\beta\le1,$$
allows noiseless recovery of $mq$, but its squared score is
$$\Lambda_\sq(\enc_\beta;mq)=K^2P\frac{\beta^2}{1+\beta^2}.$$
Its inverse conditioning therefore diverges as $\beta\downarrow0$. At zero and $K=4$, inputs $(1,1,0,0)$ and $(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2)$ give the same received sum whereas the desired function $f(\mathbf s)=mq$ takes the respective values $1/4$ and $1/8$.

For a nonconstant bounded desired function $f:I^K\to\R$, the uncentered exponential criterion assigns every encoder a positive score of at least $1/(\sup_{I^K}f-\inf_{I^K}f)^2$, even if the identifiability condition fails. Its nonzero value at zero distance therefore requires identifiability to be imposed separately. Noise-dependent exponential optimization requires a separate analysis.

\subsection{Finite Alphabets}
For $K=4$ and $s_k\in\{-1,1\}$, the possible means are $m\in\{-1,-1/2,0,1/2,1\}$ and variance is $1-m^2$. Antipodal signaling gives received sum $4\sqrt P m$, hence
$$\frac{|\Delta r|^2}{|\Delta V|^2}=\frac{16P}{(m+\widetilde m)^2}$$
whenever the two variance values differ. The largest admissible $|m+\widetilde m|$ is $3/2$, giving score $64P/9$. Every binary map has symbol separation at most $2\sqrt P$, so antipodal signaling maximizes every received gap and is globally optimal. Restricting the continuous variance-optimal parabola instead gives
$$\left(\frac4{\sqrt3}-\frac43\right)\frac{64P}{9}<\frac{64P}{9}.$$
There is no contradiction: removing source values removes the continuum's sharp shrinking pairs.
