\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{F}{unction} computation over multiple-access channels uses simultaneous transmissions to evaluate a desired function of distributed observations~\cite{nazer2007,sahin2023survey}. Conventional analog over-the-air computation combines local preprocessing of real observations, channel summation, and receiver postprocessing. Amplitude modulation implements this principle~\cite{channelcomp2024}: transmitting scaled observations, for example, enables arithmetic-mean computation. The complex baseband channel provides two real signal coordinates; the desired function remains real-valued.

Our starting point is function-dependent digital modulation, as developed in ChannelComp~\cite{channelcomp2024}. A digital encoder assigns a constellation point to each quantized observation. Their placement determines which input configurations the received sum distinguishes. Close sums associated with substantially different function values allow small disturbances to cause large computation errors. We therefore seek an input-to-symbol mapping that enables the desired computation and limits its noise sensitivity under a transmit-power constraint.

We study shared continuous maps $\enc:I\to\C$, where $I=[-1,1]\subset\R$. The transmitted symbol traces a curve in the in-phase and quadrature plane; restriction to a finite input alphabet gives a digital constellation. Its two coordinates convey two additive statistics in one complex channel use. For variance, transmitting a symbol proportional to $s+i s^2$ gives a sum proportional to $\sum_k s_k+i\sum_k s_k^2$. The receiver recovers the mean $m$ and second raw moment $q$ and computes $q-m^2$. The curve thus determines how the available signal coordinates convey the statistics needed for real-valued computation.

Transmitting $s$ and a prescribed preprocessing function $\phi(s)$ establishes achievability but leaves the optimal geometry undetermined. Their scaling controls noise sensitivity, while the pointwise power constraint couples the two coordinates. We ask whether exact computation forces every continuous encoder to preserve these sums, then optimize their scaling, relative orientation, and common offset. The input parameterization matters: encoders with the same curve image can produce different sums for the same observations.

We address these questions for real-valued desired functions $f_{\phi,\psi}:I^K\to\R$ of the form
$$
 f_{\phi,\psi}(\mathbf s)
 =\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^{K}\phi(s_k)
 -\psi\!\left(\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^{K}s_k\right),
 \qquad \mathbf s\in I^K,
$$
where $\phi,\psi:I\to\R$ are prescribed; variance corresponds to $\phi(s)=\psi(s)=s^2$. Section~\ref{sec:model} gives further examples and the relation to scalar nomographic representations. We maximize the infimum, over input pairs with unequal function values, of the squared distance between their received sums divided by their squared function difference. The inverse square root of this \emph{squared-separation score} is the smallest uniform disturbance-amplification constant achievable by an exact decoder.

\subsection{Contributions}
We connect exact computation, optimal encoder geometry, and receiver error through the following results.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Necessary encoder structure.} For $K\ge4$ under Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}'s assumptions, we prove that every continuous exact encoder has the form $\enc(s)=c+us+v\phi(s)$, where $c,u,v\in\C$ and $u,v$ are linearly independent over $\R$. It therefore preserves $S=\sum_k s_k$ and $T=\sum_k\phi(s_k)$. We derive this structure from equal-sum input triples without assuming encoder differentiability, and permit $\phi$ to have affine subintervals.
\item \emph{Optimal encoding and sharp disturbance bounds.} Under Theorem~\ref{thm:sdp}'s stronger assumptions, we express the exact score through two endpoint gradients and formulate peak-constrained design as a convex program or an equivalent enclosing-ellipse problem. We give explicit quadratic and quartic optimal encoders, then extend the characterization and convex design to two prescribed additive statistics under a common-completion identifiability condition. Examples include polynomial moment interactions such as $mq$ and a nonpolynomial transformation of variance. For exponential preprocessing, we certify a relative global gap below $0.006\%$. A fixed linear transformation, scalar clipping, and function evaluation attain the smallest uniform disturbance-amplification constant, including for nonorthogonal $u,v$.
\item \emph{A global robustness--accuracy tradeoff.} Under the stated prior assumptions and for our decoder, we minimize the leading mean-squared error (MSE) coefficient under circular complex Gaussian noise over all continuous exact encoders meeting a required score. A five-variable convex program attains the optimum and determines a unique $2\times2$ coefficient Gram matrix. The optimal value is continuous and convex in the required score, with an initial plateau and, when nonempty, a strictly increasing branch. For $\phi(s)=s^2$, an explicit scalar-root characterization describes the optimal curve's robustness--accuracy tradeoff.
\end{enumerate}
Paired finite-noise experiments and implementation-error bounds support the analysis. The appendices give detailed proofs.\ifArxivVersion{} They also establish qualitative approximate rigidity and compare other pairwise criteria.\fi

\subsection{Related Work}
Nazer and Gastpar established an information-theoretic framework for computation over multiple-access channels~\cite{nazer2007}; their compute-and-forward construction recovers linear combinations through nested lattice codes~\cite{nazer2011}. Goldenbaum, Boche, and Sta\'nczak combine nomographic representations with nested lattice coding and analyze nonlinear computation rates under a block power constraint~\cite{goldenbaum2015}. We jointly encode two prescribed real sums in one complex symbol, characterize the necessary continuous encoder structure, and optimize disturbance sensitivity under a pointwise peak constraint.

ChannelComp designs function-dependent modulation through separation constraints on received superpositions~\cite{channelcomp2024}. Variants address multiple antennas, blind federated learning, structured digital coding, and constellation sampling~\cite{veccomp2026,blindfl2024,sumcomp2025,pyramidsampling2025}. Its finite-input squared-distance objective motivates ours, but constrains the modulation-vector norm rather than each symbol's energy. Noise-aware ChannelComp adapts its criterion to the noise law~\cite{noiseaware2025}; the Gaussian criterion has an uncentered exponential numerator. Prescribed sum-computing grid constellations also admit analytical optimization with ML or MAP decoding~\cite{towards2025}. We complement these digital designs with a continuous encoder converse and a global squared-separation optimum for the stated nonlinear function classes.

Shared sum representations underlie Deep Sets~\cite{zaheer2017}. Wagstaff \emph{et al.}\ establish latent-dimension requirements for universal continuous representation and approximation~\cite{wagstaff19,wagstaff22}. Their exact-max argument combines equal-sum analysis with topology, including fixed cardinality~\cite[Thm. 14 and Lemma 17]{wagstaff22}; particular desired functions may admit smaller representations. Chen, Chen, and Lu use multisymmetric polynomial generators and examine postprocessing singularities~\cite{chen2024}.

The closest affine-uniqueness precedent is Charalambides' restricted Cauchy--Pexider theorem~\cite[Theorem 1.6]{charalambides2013}. For smooth curves without affine subarcs, it identifies affine summands from an additive factorization by regularizing before differentiation. We derive the local factorization from exact scalar-function computation and permit $C^1$ curves with affine subintervals. We prove this reduction self-containedly; affine uniqueness itself is established. Scalar monotone-generator uniqueness for quasi-arithmetic means provides another precedent~\cite[Theorems A and D]{matkowski2015}.

Our small-noise objective has the weighted inverse-trace form of A-optimal experimental design. Conic formulations with resource constraints are classical~\cite{sagnol2010}, including semidefinite methods for continuous polynomial and rational design spaces~\cite{papp2011}. After characterizing exact encoders, we apply these established matrix and enclosing-ellipsoid tools~\cite{boyd2004} to affine images of $(s,\phi(s))$ inside a peak-power disk. This yields a global robustness--accuracy tradeoff for our computation class, decoder, and small-noise model.

We use classical invariance of domain~\cite{hatcher} and scalar Lipschitz extension~\cite{mcshane1934} to establish the encoder characterization and its operational interpretation.

Section~\ref{sec:pareto} gives the small-noise tradeoff; Sections~\ref{sec:certified} and~\ref{sec:evidence} address numerical certification and performance. The appendices contain proofs and additional consequences.
