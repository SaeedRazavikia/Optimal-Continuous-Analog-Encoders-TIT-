\section{Encoder Design}\label{sec:design}
We first identify the encoder family required for exact computation and then optimize its geometry under the peak constraint.

\subsection{Exact Encoders}\label{sec:rigidity}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:rigidity}
Let $K\ge4$. Assume $\phi\in C(I)\cap C^1((-1,1))$ is nonaffine and $\psi\in C(I)\cap C^1((-1,1))$ is strictly convex or strictly concave. A continuous planar encoder exactly computes \eqref{eq:target} if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \enc(s)=c+u s+v\phi(s),
 \qquad c,u,v\in\R^2,
 \label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
with $u,v$ linearly independent in the real plane.
\end{theorem}
The proof appears in Appendix~\ref{app:rigidity-proof}. Its key step uses a fourth transmitter to show that equal encoded triple sums must preserve both aggregate features. The receiver can then recover $S,T$ from $r_\enc-Kc=uS+vT$ and evaluate the target. Thus exact computation leaves the scale, orientation, and offset of the feature curve available for noise-aware design.

The classification uses one complex channel use and $K\ge4$. It does not settle the unrestricted $K=3$ case. Its assumptions permit unbounded endpoint derivatives and therefore do not alone imply positive separation.
