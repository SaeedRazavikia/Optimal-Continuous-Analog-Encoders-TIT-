\section{Encoder Design}\label{sec:design}
We first characterize the continuous encoders that permit identifiable computation of the desired function. This characterization reduces the search over arbitrary curves to an optimization of their geometry under the peak constraint.

\subsection{Encoder Characterization}\label{sec:rigidity}
A real-valued desired function $f:I^K\to\R$ might appear to require less information than its two defining statistics $S$ and $T$. The next theorem rules out that possibility under regime R: identifiable computation of $f$ for every input vector forces the shared encoder to preserve both sums. This gives the converse needed to compare a proposed curve with all continuous alternatives.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:rigidity}
Let $K\ge4$. Assume $\phi\in C(I)\cap C^1((-1,1))$ is nonaffine and $\psi\in C(I)\cap C^1((-1,1))$ is strictly convex or strictly concave. A continuous encoder $\enc:I\to\R^2\cong\C$ satisfies the identifiability condition \eqref{eq:exactness} for $f_{\phi,\psi}:I^K\to\R$ in \eqref{eq:target} if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \enc(s)=c+u s+v\phi(s),
 \qquad s\in I,\quad c,u,v\in\R^2,
 \label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
with $u,v$ linearly independent, equivalently $\det[\,u\ v\,]\ne0$.
\end{theorem}
The proof appears in Appendix~\ref{app:rigidity-proof}. Its key step uses a fourth transmitter to show that equal encoded triple sums must preserve both sums $S$ and $T$. The receiver can then recover $S,T$ from $r_\enc-Kc=uS+vT$ and evaluate $f_{\phi,\psi}$. The theorem therefore fixes the preprocessing curve $(s,\phi(s))$, while leaving its scale, orientation, and offset available for design in the presence of noise.

The classification uses one complex channel use and $K\ge4$. It does not establish the necessity of \eqref{eq:affine} for unrestricted continuous encoders when $K=3$.\footnote{For $K=3$, a collision between two encoded triples implies only $T-\widetilde T=3[\psi(S/3)-\psi(\widetilde S/3)]$; changes in the two statistics can cancel in this identity. For $K\ge4$, we append the same fourth input $w\in I$ and $K-4$ zeros to both triples. The identifiability condition then gives $T-\widetilde T=K[\psi((S+w)/K)-\psi((\widetilde S+w)/K)]$ for every $w\in I$. Comparing $w=0$ and $w=1/2$, strict convexity or concavity forces $S=\widetilde S$, and hence $T=\widetilde T$; see Lemma~\ref{lem:collision}. There is no fourth input to vary when $K=3$, so this proof does not establish preservation of both sums in that case. The affine form with independent $u,v$ remains sufficient for identifiable computation when $K=3$; only its necessity is unresolved here.} Its assumptions permit unbounded endpoint derivatives and therefore do not alone imply positive separation.
