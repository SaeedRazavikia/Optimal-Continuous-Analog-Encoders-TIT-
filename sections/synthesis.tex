\subsection{Optimal Design}\label{sec:synthesis}
Having identified the necessary local preprocessing functions $s$ and $\phi(s)$, we now optimize their coefficient vectors $u,v\in\R^2$. We assume $\phi\in C(I)\cap C^1((-1,1))$ and $\psi\in C^1(I)$, each strictly convex or strictly concave. Finite endpoint slopes of the mean-dependent term provide a uniform disturbance guarantee; $\phi$ needs differentiability only in the interval interior. Let $\mathbb S^n$ denote the real symmetric $n\times n$ matrices, and let $\mathbb S^n_+$ and $\mathbb S^n_{++}$ denote its positive semidefinite and positive definite subsets; $H\succeq0$ and $H\succ0$ mean positive semidefinite and positive definite, respectively. For the encoder coefficients in \eqref{eq:affine}, we define
\begin{align*}
 G&=\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\ C&B\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb S^2_{++},\quad
 w_\pm=\begin{pmatrix}-\psi'(\pm1)\\1\end{pmatrix}\in\R^2,\\
 A&=|u|^2,\quad B=|v|^2,\quad C=u^\T v=\operatorname{Re}(u\overline v),
\end{align*}
where the complex expression for $C$ uses the identification $\R^2\cong\C$. The scalars $A,B$ are the squared lengths of $u,v$, and $C$ is their real inner product. The Gram matrix $G$ therefore records the part of the transmit geometry that affects received distances; $C=0$ means orthogonal directions. The vectors $w_\pm$ describe the sensitivity of $f_{\phi,\psi}$ at the extreme input means.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:sdp}
Let $K\ge4$ and $P>0$, and impose the assumptions of this subsection. For every encoder \eqref{eq:affine} with independent $u,v$,
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_{\sq}(\enc;f_{\phi,\psi})
 =\frac{K^2}{\max\{w_-^\T G^{-1}w_-,\;w_+^\T G^{-1}w_+\}}.
 \label{eq:exactscore}
\end{equation}
The infimum is approached at arbitrarily small received distance. The global optimum over all continuous peak-admissible encoders is $K^2\lambda_\star$, where $\lambda_\star$ is the optimum of
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \text{maximize}\quad &\lambda\\
 \text{over}\quad &H\in\mathbb S^3,\quad\lambda\ge0,\\
 \text{subject to}\quad
 &H\suc0,\\
 &z(s)^\T H z(s)\le P\quad\text{for every }s\in[-1,1],\\
 &G-\lambda w_-w_-^\T\suc0,\\
 &G-\lambda w_+w_+^\T\suc0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sdp}
\end{equation}
where $z(s)=(1,s,\phi(s))^\T\in\R^3$ and $G$ is the lower-right $2\times2$ block of $H$. This is a convex semidefinite problem with seven real variables and a continuum of scalar peak constraints. Its optimum is attained and yields an exact planar encoder.
\end{theorem}
See Appendix~\ref{app:design-proof} for the score calculation and the proof of optimality and attainment. Equation~\eqref{eq:exactscore} separates two design roles: $\phi$ determines the feasible peak geometry, while $\psi$ enters through its two extreme slopes.

The score balances sensitivity of $f_{\phi,\psi}$ against the received distance available in the directions $u$ and $v$. Enlarging a direction can reduce its noise amplification, but the peak constraint couples both directions through the entire curve. The semidefinite formulation performs this allocation jointly with the curve's offset.

To implement a solution, write $H=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}h&\ell^\T\\\ell&G\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, choose an invertible $L\in\R^{2\times2}$ with $L^{\T}L=G$, and set $c=(L^\T)^{-1}\ell$. The columns of $L$ give $u,v$. Reducing $h$ to $\ell^{\T}G^{-1}\ell$ preserves the score and can only lower the peak energy, so a planar encoder always suffices.

The peak constraint must hold between samples. Section~\ref{sec:examples} gives explicit curves with analytic certificates, and Section~\ref{sec:certified} provides certified numerical design for other smooth preprocessing functions $\phi$.
