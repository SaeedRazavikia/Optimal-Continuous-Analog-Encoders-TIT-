## Independence test

The TIT paper should remain a complete argument after removing the Gaussian exponential certificates. Its central question is how a prescribed computation forces a curve and how to optimize that forced geometry.

The TCOM paper should retain a substantive independent contribution after the moment-rigidity and squared-separation results are cited as prior or companion work. Its central question is how a noise-dependent computation criterion yields useful analog designs, certified regimes, and principled selection among competing or tied curves.

A different metric alone does not guarantee enough independent novelty. Do not split one proof into two papers that cannot stand alone. A shared model, common notation, or the same example is acceptable when each manuscript has a clearly distinct contribution and proper cross-reference.

## How to report overlap

Maintain a theorem-level overlap table listing the exact target, criterion, admissible encoder class, source domain, power constraint, result type, proof dependency, and primary manuscript. Disclose related manuscripts when preparing eventual submission materials. No submission is requested in this handoff.

If a result changes during research, update both papers' boundary records. If one manuscript depends on an unpublished companion result, provide the exact statement and a reviewable proof source; do not conceal that dependency.
