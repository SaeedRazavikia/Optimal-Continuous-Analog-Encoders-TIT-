Dear Editor,

Please consider “Optimal Continuous Analog Encoders for Moment-Based Computation” for publication in *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory*.

The paper asks which continuous modulation curves can compute a prescribed nonlinear aggregate through a single complex multiple-access channel use, and how to design those curves under a per-transmitter peak constraint. For at least four transmitters, we prove that exact computation of the stated moment-minus-mean targets forces the encoder to be an affine image of a two-feature curve. This necessity result turns an optimization over continuous functions into an attained finite-dimensional convex design problem.

We derive the exact worst-case squared-separation score, construct a decoder attaining its uniform disturbance constant, and obtain explicit quadratic and quartic optimal encoders. We then characterize the globally optimal leading small-noise MSE coefficient, for the specified decoder and source law, under a prescribed robustness floor. The convex frontier has a plateau and an active increasing branch; the variance case has an explicit scalar-root characterization. A certified exponential example and paired numerical results illustrate the guarantees.

We distinguish this computation-specific contribution from established functional-equation regularization, topology, Lipschitz extension, and convex design methods. The main significance is the complete link from scalar exactness to necessary transmitted features and globally optimal robust design, including an explicit robustness–accuracy tradeoff. We do not claim unrestricted finite-noise Bayesian optimality.

The manuscript includes editable native TikZ/PGFPlots figures, and its numerical code and supporting data accompany the source package. The acknowledgment identifies the substantive use of OpenAI ChatGPT/Codex in the development and presentation of the work.
