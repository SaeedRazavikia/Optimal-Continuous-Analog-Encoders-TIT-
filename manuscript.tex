\begin{abstract}
Function computation over multiple-access channels uses simultaneous transmissions to evaluate a desired function of distributed observations. Variance, for example, can be computed from the sum of the observations and the sum of their squares. Motivated by digital constellation design, we represent each bounded real observation by a point on a continuous curve in the complex plane, whose two coordinates can convey these sums in one channel use. We study which curves permit exact computation without noise and how to design them for robustness to noise under a power limit for every input. For a class of moment-based functions that includes variance, we prove that every exact shared continuous encoder must preserve two prescribed sums when there are at least four transmitters and the stated regularity and convexity conditions hold. This characterization yields a convex optimization problem for the globally optimal encoder under a worst-case separation criterion, which compares received distances with differences in desired function values. We derive explicit quadratic and quartic curves and extend the framework to functions of two prescribed additive statistics. A projection decoder attains the smallest uniform noise-amplification constant. For a specified input distribution and this decoder, we also characterize the tradeoff between worst-case separation and small-noise mean-squared error.
\end{abstract}
